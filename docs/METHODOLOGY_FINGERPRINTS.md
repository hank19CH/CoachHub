## 1. Charlie Francis (High-Low)

**Domain:** Sprint/Power Athletics (100m, 200m, jumps, throws)
**Primary Source:** "Training for Speed" (Charlie Francis, 1997), CFTS Manual, "The Charlie Francis Training System"

### 1.1 Intensity Distribution

| Zone | % of Total Sessions | Intensity Range |
|------|-------------------|-----------------|
| HIGH (CNS) | 30-40% | 95-100% max velocity / >85% 1RM |
| LOW (regeneration) | 60-70% | <75% max velocity / <70% 1RM |
| MEDIUM | 0% | 75-95% (explicitly forbidden) |

**Critical:** The complete absence of medium-intensity work is THE defining characteristic. Francis called the medium zone the "no-man's land" -- too fast to recover from but too slow to produce adaptation.

### 1.2 Session Type Mix (Typical Week)

| Day | Type | Content |
|-----|------|---------|
| Monday | HIGH | Speed work (30-60m sprints at 95-100%), plyometrics, max strength (squats/bench 3-5 reps at 85-95%) |
| Tuesday | LOW | Tempo runs (100-300m at 65-75% max velocity), general strength circuits (body weight, med ball), extensive stretching |
| Wednesday | HIGH | Speed endurance (80-150m at 95-100%), Olympic lifts or speed-strength, plyometrics |
| Thursday | LOW | Tempo runs, pool recovery, massage, light circuits |
| Friday | HIGH | Speed (acceleration work 10-40m), max strength (deadlifts, cleans at 85-95%) |
| Saturday | LOW | Tempo runs, regeneration |
| Sunday | OFF | Complete rest |

**Frequency:** 3 HIGH days + 3 LOW days + 1 rest = 7 days
**Sprint volume per HIGH day:** 200-600m total (elite level), never exceeding quality threshold
**Tempo volume per LOW day:** 1500-3000m total at 65-75% max velocity

### 1.3 Volume-Intensity Relationship

**INVERSE** -- but uniquely structured. Volume is strictly controlled on HIGH days (short, explosive, full recovery). Volume is intentionally high on LOW days (2000-3000m of tempo work). As competition approaches, HIGH day volume decreases while intensity approaches 100%. LOW day volume remains relatively constant throughout.

- GPP: HIGH day sprint volume ~500-600m, LOW day tempo ~2500-3000m
- SPP: HIGH day sprint volume ~300-400m, LOW day tempo ~2000-2500m
- Competition: HIGH day sprint volume ~150-250m, LOW day tempo ~1500-2000m

### 1.4 Deload Pattern

- **Frequency:** Every 3 weeks (3:1 loading pattern typical, sometimes 2:1)
- **Volume reduction:** 30-50% reduction in HIGH day sprint volume
- **Unique characteristic:** LOW day tempo volume reduces only ~10-20% during deload -- the recovery work continues
- **Pre-competition taper:** 10-14 days, HIGH day volume drops 60-70%, intensity maintained at 100%

### 1.5 Progression Model

**STEP LOADING with horizontal accumulation:**
- Week 1: Baseline volume/intensity
- Week 2: +10-15% sprint volume OR +5% intensity (never both simultaneously)
- Week 3: Peak loading (highest stress)
- Week 4: Deload (30-50% reduction in sprint volume)
- Next cycle starts at Week 1 level slightly above previous cycle's Week 1

Key rule: Volume and intensity NEVER increase simultaneously within the same week. One goes up, the other stays constant or drops.

### 1.6 Recovery Spacing

- **Between HIGH days:** Strictly 48 hours minimum, 72 hours preferred
- **HIGH days never on consecutive days** -- this is an absolute rule
- **LOW days always between HIGH days** -- they serve as active recovery
- **Within HIGH sessions:** Full recovery between sprint reps (3-7 minutes for short sprints, up to 10-15 min for speed endurance)

### 1.7 Unique Discriminatory Markers

1. **ZERO medium-intensity sessions** -- No sessions at 75-95% max effort. This is unique to Francis.
2. **Tempo runs at precisely 65-75%** -- Not 80%, not 60%. The range is specific.
3. **CNS-based day classification** -- Days categorized by neural demand, not muscle group
4. **Sprint volume caps per session** -- Never exceeding 600m total sprint work per HIGH session
5. **Weights paired with sprints on same day** -- Heavy lifting always on HIGH days, NEVER on LOW days
6. **Fixed 48-72hr CNS recovery windows** -- HIGH days always separated by exactly 1-2 LOW days

### 1.8 Hard Exclusion Rules

- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Any session exists at 80-90% intensity (medium zone)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Heavy lifting appears on LOW/tempo days
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Two HIGH days are consecutive (e.g., Mon-Tue both high CNS)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Sprint volumes exceed 800m in a single session
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Tempo runs are faster than 75% max velocity
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** No explicit LOW/tempo/recovery days between HIGH days
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Endurance runs exceed 30 minutes continuous

### 1.9 Typical Block/Phase Structure

| Phase | Duration | Focus |
|-------|----------|-------|
| GPP (General Prep) | 6-8 weeks | Tempo volume building, general strength, acceleration mechanics, 3 HIGH + 3 LOW |
| SPP I (Special Prep) | 4-6 weeks | Speed endurance development, max strength, plyometric intensity increases |
| SPP II (Pre-Competition) | 3-4 weeks | Competition-specific distances, speed maintenance, strength maintenance |
| Competition | 6-12 weeks | Performance peaking, minimal volume, maximal intensity, 2-3 HIGH + 3-4 LOW |

**Total macrocycle:** 20-30 weeks (single periodization for outdoor track season)

### 1.10 Common Variations

- **Pure Francis:** Strict high-low with zero medium work. Track + weights on same HIGH day.
- **Modified Francis (team sport):** HIGH days may include sport practice. LOW days include tempo + sport-specific low-intensity drills. Medium-intensity sport practice may creep in (violates purity).
- **Concurrent Francis:** Some coaches add a moderate strength day (70-80%) on LOW days -- this deviates from pure Francis.
- **Short-to-long vs Long-to-short:** Francis used short-to-long (build from 30m to 60m to 100m). Some coaches reverse this. Both maintain high-low structure.

## 2. Arthur Lydiard (Base Building)

**Domain:** Distance Running (800m to Marathon)
**Primary Source:** "Running to the Top" (Arthur Lydiard, 1995), "Running with Lydiard" (Lydiard & Gilmour), "Lore of Running" (Noakes, documenting Lydiard)

### 2.1 Intensity Distribution

| Zone | % of Sessions (Base Phase) | % of Sessions (Sharpening) |
|------|---------------------------|---------------------------|
| LOW (aerobic, <75% HRmax) | 85-95% | 50-60% |
| MEDIUM (threshold/tempo, 75-88% HRmax) | 5-10% | 15-20% |
| HIGH (intervals/racing, >88% HRmax) | 0-5% | 25-30% |

**Critical:** During the base building phase (the longest phase), virtually ALL running is aerobic. The aerobic dominance during base is the defining fingerprint.

### 2.2 Session Type Mix

**Base Building Phase (10-14 weeks):**

| Day | Session | Volume |
|-----|---------|--------|
| Monday | Long aerobic run | 22-35 km (60-75% effort) |
| Tuesday | Easy aerobic run | 13-19 km (60-70% effort) |
| Wednesday | Medium-long aerobic | 16-22 km (60-75% effort) |
| Thursday | Easy aerobic run | 13-16 km (60-70% effort) |
| Friday | Medium aerobic run | 13-19 km (60-70% effort) |
| Saturday | Easy/recovery run | 10-13 km (55-65% effort) |
| Sunday | Long aerobic run | 25-35 km (65-75% effort) |

**Weekly mileage:** 100-160 km/week (60-100+ miles/week) during base phase

**Hill Phase (4-6 weeks):**
- 3x/week hill circuits (bounding, springing, steep hills)
- 4x/week aerobic runs
- Weekly mileage: 80-120 km/week

**Sharpening/Anaerobic Phase (4-6 weeks):**
- 2-3x/week track sessions (intervals, repetitions)
- Remaining days aerobic
- Weekly mileage: 70-100 km/week

### 2.3 Volume-Intensity Relationship

**SEQUENTIAL, NOT INVERSE within a week.** Volume is maximized first (base), then intensity is introduced as volume decreases in later phases. Within the base phase, intensity is essentially constant (all easy). The relationship is:

- Base: VERY HIGH volume, VERY LOW intensity
- Hills: HIGH volume, LOW-MEDIUM intensity
- Anaerobic: MEDIUM volume, MEDIUM-HIGH intensity
- Racing: LOW volume, HIGH intensity

Across the macrocycle: classic inverse relationship (volume down, intensity up). But within any given week during base, there's no volume-intensity trade-off because intensity is uniformly low.

### 2.4 Deload Pattern

- **Lydiard did NOT use formal deload weeks** -- this is distinctive
- **Recovery was built into daily/weekly rhythm:** Easy days between harder days, long run followed by easy day
- **Volume reduction:** Occurs naturally as phases transition (base to hills = ~20% volume drop, hills to anaerobic = ~20% more)
- **No systematic 3:1 or 4:1 loading cycles** -- Lydiard believed consistent aerobic volume built fitness without needing deloads

### 2.5 Progression Model

**PHASE-SEQUENTIAL (not wave or linear within phases):**
1. Base building: Volume gradually increases over 10-14 weeks from 80 km/week to 140+ km/week (linear increase)
2. Hills: Volume drops ~20%, hill work intensity increases
3. Anaerobic: Volume drops ~20% more, track intervals introduced
4. Coordination/speed: Short reps, race-pace work
5. Taper/Racing: Volume drops significantly (50%+)

Within the base phase, progression is purely volume-based: add 5-10 km/week until target mileage is reached. Pace is NOT a progression variable during base.

### 2.6 Recovery Spacing

- **Easy-hard alternation:** Hard/long days followed by easy/short days
- **No strict hour-based recovery windows** (unlike Francis)
- **Double sessions:** 2 runs/day common at high mileage (AM easy 8-10 km, PM main session)
- **Complete rest days:** Rare in Lydiard system. Active recovery (easy jog) preferred.
- **Between high-intensity sessions (anaerobic phase):** 48-72 hours, with aerobic running between

### 2.7 Unique Discriminatory Markers

1. **100+ miles/week aerobic base phase lasting 10-14 weeks** -- No other methodology prescribes this volume for this duration at purely aerobic intensity
2. **Hill circuit phase as a distinct block** -- Specific bounding/springing drills on hills, not just "hill repeats"
3. **NO intensity during base building** -- Even tempo runs are avoided during base (unlike Polarized which includes some high-intensity during base period)
4. **No formal deload weeks** -- Recovery embedded in daily easy/hard rhythm
5. **Phase transitions are sharp, not gradual** -- Base -> Hills is a distinct shift, not a gradual blend
6. **7 days/week running** -- Rest days are extremely rare in Lydiard programming
7. **Long runs up to 35 km (22+ miles)** -- At easy pace, 2x per week during peak base

### 2.8 Hard Exclusion Rules

- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Interval/speed sessions appear during weeks 1-10 of the program (base phase violation)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Weekly mileage is below 60 km/week during base phase (too low for Lydiard)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Program has structured deload weeks (Lydiard doesn't use them)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** More than 2 high-intensity sessions per week appear (even in anaerobic phase)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Program includes significant cross-training or non-running days
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Base phase is shorter than 8 weeks
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Program follows a strength/power training emphasis (Lydiard is running-only)

### 2.9 Typical Block/Phase Structure

| Phase | Duration | Weekly Mileage | Key Sessions |
|-------|----------|---------------|--------------|
| Base Building / Aerobic Conditioning | 10-14 weeks | 100-160 km | All aerobic runs, 2 long runs, doubles |
| Hill Resistance | 4-6 weeks | 80-120 km | 3 hill circuits + aerobic runs |
| Anaerobic Development (Track) | 4-6 weeks | 70-100 km | Intervals (200-1600m), time trials, aerobic runs |
| Coordination / Speed | 2-4 weeks | 60-80 km | Fast relaxed striders, race-pace work, tune-up races |
| Taper & Racing | 2-4 weeks | 40-60 km | Easy running, 1-2 sharpening sessions, target race |

**Total macrocycle:** 24-34 weeks (single macrocycle per competitive season)

### 2.10 Common Variations

- **Pure Lydiard:** 100+ miles/week base, NO intensity until hill phase. Very few coaches implement this purely.
- **Modern Lydiard (Renato Canova influence):** Adds some tempo/fartlek during late base phase. Base mileage may be 70-90 miles/week instead of 100+.
- **Daniels-Lydiard hybrid:** Uses Lydiard's base volume concept but introduces threshold runs during base per Jack Daniels's V-dot system.
- **Shortened Lydiard:** 6-8 week base (recreational runners). Loses some aerobic depth but maintains phase structure.
- **Marathon-specific Lydiard:** Base extends to 16-20 weeks. Hill phase shortened. Long run emphasis increased.

## 3. Polarized Training (Seiler)

**Domain:** Endurance Sports (running, cycling, rowing, XC skiing, swimming)
**Primary Source:** Stephen Seiler's research papers (2010-2019), published in MSSE, IJSPP, and Frontiers in Physiology. "The Training Intensity Distribution Among Well-Trained and Elite Endurance Athletes" (Seiler & Kjerland, 2006).

### 3.1 Intensity Distribution

| Zone | % of Total Training Volume | Heart Rate / Lactate |
|------|--------------------------|---------------------|
| Zone 1 (LOW) | 75-85% | <VT1 (below first ventilatory threshold), <2 mmol/L lactate, <80% HRmax |
| Zone 2 (MODERATE/Threshold) | 0-5% | VT1-VT2 (between thresholds), 2-4 mmol/L, 80-88% HRmax |
| Zone 3 (HIGH) | 15-20% | >VT2 (above second ventilatory threshold), >4 mmol/L, >88% HRmax |

**Critical:** The "polarized" fingerprint is the AVOIDANCE of Zone 2, not merely doing lots of easy work. Zone 2 (threshold/tempo) accounts for less than 5% of total training. This distinguishes it from both Lydiard (which also does lots of easy but in sequential phases) and Norwegian (which deliberately targets Zone 2).

### 3.2 Session Type Mix (Typical Week)

| Day | Type | Details |
|-----|------|---------|
| Monday | Zone 1 Easy | 45-90 min easy aerobic |
| Tuesday | Zone 3 HIGH | Intervals: 4-6 x 4-8 min at >90% HRmax (VO2max intervals), or 8-12 x 2-3 min |
| Wednesday | Zone 1 Easy | 45-90 min recovery/easy |
| Thursday | Zone 1 Easy | 45-90 min easy aerobic |
| Friday | Zone 3 HIGH | Intervals: 5-8 x 3-5 min at 90-95% HRmax, or tempo-like continuous at 85-88% |
| Saturday | Zone 1 Easy/Long | 90-150 min long easy aerobic |
| Sunday | Zone 1 Easy | 30-60 min recovery |

**Frequency:** 2-3 HIGH sessions/week + 4-5 LOW sessions/week
**Total volume:** Varies by sport and athlete level; elite runners 80-130 km/week

### 3.3 Volume-Intensity Relationship

**PARALLEL MAINTENANCE with seasonal variation:**
- Both high volume of Zone 1 AND high-intensity Zone 3 sessions are maintained throughout the training year
- This is NOT sequential like Lydiard (where intensity comes after volume)
- Volume (Zone 1) and intensity (Zone 3) coexist week-to-week year-round
- Some seasonal adjustment: competition period may reduce Zone 1 volume slightly while maintaining Zone 3 quality

### 3.4 Deload Pattern

- **Not rigidly prescribed** in Seiler's research
- **Common practice:** Every 3-4 weeks (3:1 or 4:1 loading)
- **Volume reduction:** 20-30% reduction in Zone 1 volume
- **Zone 3 reduction:** Reduce to 1 high-intensity session (from 2-3) during deload weeks
- **Characteristic:** Total training hours drop, but the few sessions that remain still follow the polarized distribution

### 3.5 Progression Model

**THRESHOLD-BASED (not linear volume progression):**
- Zone 1 base established first (4-6 weeks building to target volume)
- Zone 3 sessions introduced at 2/week, may progress to 3/week in peak periods
- Interval session intensity progresses: longer intervals at moderate Zone 3 -> shorter intervals at higher Zone 3 -> race-specific intervals
- Volume progression is moderate and gradual (5-10% per week at most)
- The model is more about MAINTAINING the distribution than linearly increasing anything

### 3.6 Recovery Spacing

- **Between Zone 3 sessions:** 48-72 hours mandatory
- **Zone 1 sessions serve as recovery** between Zone 3 days (similar concept to Francis LOW days)
- **No Zone 3 sessions on consecutive days**
- **A Zone 1 day always follows a Zone 3 day**
- **Doubles:** Common at higher volumes; second session always Zone 1

### 3.7 Unique Discriminatory Markers

1. **Zone 2 avoidance (<5% of volume)** -- This is the PRIMARY discriminator. Most other methods spend 15-40% in Zone 2.
2. **Three-zone model** -- Specifically uses VT1/VT2 demarcation, not pace-based
3. **Year-round high-intensity presence** -- Unlike Lydiard, Zone 3 work is present in ALL phases (just reduced, never eliminated)
4. **75-85% Zone 1 volume** -- Higher than most methods' easy volume
5. **2-3 high-intensity sessions per week, consistently** -- Not periodized heavily
6. **Sessions are classified by zone, not by "type"** -- No "tempo runs" -- they'd fall in the forbidden Zone 2

### 3.8 Hard Exclusion Rules

- **DISQUALIFIED if:** More than 10% of sessions are in Zone 2 (threshold/tempo)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** "Tempo runs" at 80-88% HRmax appear as a regular weekly session
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** A multi-week phase exists with NO high-intensity sessions (Lydiard base pattern)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** The intensity distribution shows a bell curve (most training at moderate intensity)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** More than 3 Zone 3 sessions per week regularly
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Program uses 5-zone or 7-zone intensity scales (Polarized specifically uses 3 zones)

### 3.9 Typical Block/Phase Structure

Polarized training is **minimally periodized** by design:

| Phase | Duration | Z1/Z2/Z3 Split | Notes |
|-------|----------|----------------|-------|
| General Prep | 4-8 weeks | 85/5/10 | Volume building, 2 Zone 3 sessions/week |
| Specific Prep | 8-12 weeks | 80/5/15 | 2-3 Zone 3 sessions/week, race-specific intervals |
| Competition | 4-8 weeks | 75/5/20 | 2-3 Zone 3 sessions/week, intervals become more race-specific |
| Transition | 2-4 weeks | 90/5/5 | Recovery, reduced volume, 1 Zone 3 session/week |

**Key:** The distribution barely changes across phases. This is a defining feature.

### 3.10 Common Variations

- **Pure Seiler Polarized:** Strict <5% Zone 2. Monitoring via session HR or lactate.
- **Pyramidal (near-polarized):** Zone 2 rises to 10-15%, creating a pyramidal distribution (Z1 > Z2 > Z3). Many elite endurance athletes actually train this way, though they call it "polarized."
- **Modified polarized for time-crunched athletes:** Fewer total sessions but maintaining the 80/20 ratio. May result in 4-5 sessions/week (3 easy + 1-2 hard).
- **Triathlon polarized:** Each discipline follows 80/20 independently.

## 4. Norwegian Method (Double Threshold)

**Domain:** Distance Running (primarily 1500m-10,000m, extending to half/full marathon)
**Primary Source:** Research from the Norwegian Olympic Training Center (Olympiatoppen), Marius Bakken's publications, case studies of Jakob Ingebrigtsen's training (Gjert Ingebrigtsen coaching), Casado et al. (2023) "Training Characteristics of World-Class Distance Runners."

### 4.1 Intensity Distribution

| Zone | % of Total Sessions | Intensity |
|------|-------------------|-----------|
| Zone 1 (Easy) | 55-65% | <LT1 (below lactate threshold 1), ~2 mmol/L |
| Zone 2 (Threshold) | 25-35% | LT1-LT2, 2-4 mmol/L (the TARGET zone) |
| Zone 3 (High/VO2max) | 5-15% | >LT2, >4 mmol/L |

**Critical:** The Norwegian Method is defined by the HIGH proportion of threshold work (25-35%), which is the exact zone that Polarized training avoids. This is the single strongest discriminator between these two methods.

### 4.2 Session Type Mix (Typical Week)

| Day | AM Session | PM Session |
|-----|------------|------------|
| Monday | Easy run 40-60 min | Threshold session: 5-6 x 6 min at LT2 pace (~4 mmol/L) |
| Tuesday | Easy run 40-50 min | Easy run 40-50 min |
| Wednesday | Threshold session: 4-5 x 2000m at LT2 | Easy run 40-50 min |
| Thursday | Easy run 40-60 min | Threshold session: 3-4 x 10 min at LT1-LT2 |
| Friday | Easy run 30-40 min | Easy run 40-50 min |
| Saturday | Long run 80-100 min (aerobic) | Rest or easy jog 20-30 min |
| Sunday | VO2max intervals: 5 x 3 min at >95% HRmax | Rest |

**Frequency:** 10-14 sessions/week (doubles nearly every day)
**Threshold sessions:** 3-5 per week (THIS IS THE FINGERPRINT)
**VO2max sessions:** 0-1 per week

### 4.3 Volume-Intensity Relationship

**ADDITIVE (not inverse):** Both volume AND threshold intensity are maintained at high levels simultaneously. This is a concurrent loading model where:
- Total weekly volume: 130-180 km/week (elite level)
- Threshold volume within that: 30-50 km/week at threshold pace
- Easy volume: 80-120 km/week
- VO2max volume: 5-15 km/week

The Norwegian approach does NOT periodize by reducing volume to increase intensity. They increase BOTH over time, limited by recovery capacity.

### 4.4 Deload Pattern

- **Frequency:** Every 2-3 weeks (2:1 or 3:1 loading)
- **Volume reduction:** 20-30% total volume reduction
- **Threshold reduction:** Drop from 4-5 threshold sessions to 2-3 during deload
- **Unique:** Lactate monitoring guides deload timing -- if threshold lactate values drift upward or pace at target lactate drops, deload is triggered regardless of schedule
- **Recovery weeks maintain some threshold work** -- never eliminated entirely

### 4.5 Progression Model

**LACTATE-GUIDED UNDULATING:**
- Primary metric: pace at 4 mmol/L blood lactate
- Progression = same pace produces lower lactate, or faster pace at same lactate
- Threshold interval length progresses: 4-5 min reps -> 6-8 min reps -> 10-15 min reps
- Volume of threshold work per session increases gradually: 20 min -> 25 min -> 30 min total threshold time
- NOT percentage-based or pace-based -- all guided by real-time or recent lactate testing

### 4.6 Recovery Spacing

- **Between threshold sessions:** As little as 12-24 hours (the "double threshold" concept)
- **Double threshold days:** AM + PM threshold sessions separated by 6-8 hours
- **VO2max sessions:** 48-72 hours between, maximum 1 per week
- **Complete rest days:** 0-1 per week (very rare)
- **The short recovery between threshold sessions is deliberate** -- it creates a cumulative threshold stimulus without the CNS fatigue of VO2max work

### 4.7 Unique Discriminatory Markers

1. **Double threshold days** -- Two threshold sessions in one day (AM + PM). NO other method does this.
2. **25-35% of training at threshold** -- This is 5-7x more threshold work than Polarized training
3. **Lactate-guided prescription** -- Sessions defined by blood lactate targets (e.g., "6 x 6 min at 3.5-4.0 mmol/L"), not pace or HR
4. **10-14 sessions per week** -- Doubles nearly every day, much higher frequency than most methods
5. **Very low VO2max session frequency** -- Only 0-1 per week despite being for 1500-10k runners
6. **Threshold sessions are never eliminated** -- Present in every training phase year-round
7. **Short recovery tolerance between threshold sessions** -- 12-24 hours vs 48+ hours for all other methods

### 4.8 Hard Exclusion Rules

- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Fewer than 3 threshold sessions per week
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** No double-session days exist in the program
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Threshold work is less than 20% of total volume
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** More than 2 VO2max sessions per week (Norwegian limits these)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Program doesn't include lactate references (mmol/L, blood lactate, LT1, LT2)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Training has fewer than 8 sessions per week (Norwegian demands high frequency)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** A multi-week period has zero threshold work (Norwegian never eliminates it)

### 4.9 Typical Block/Phase Structure

| Phase | Duration | Threshold Sessions/Week | VO2max Sessions/Week |
|-------|----------|------------------------|---------------------|
| Base / Foundation | 6-10 weeks | 3-4 (shorter intervals, lower volume) | 0-1 |
| Build / Development | 8-12 weeks | 4-5 (longer intervals, higher threshold volume) | 1 |
| Specific / Pre-Competition | 4-6 weeks | 3-4 (race-specific intensities mixed in) | 1-2 |
| Competition / Peaking | 2-4 weeks | 2-3 (reduced volume, maintained intensity) | 1 |

**Key:** Phase transitions are subtle -- threshold work is present in all phases. The method is more "constant" than "periodized."

### 4.10 Common Variations

- **Pure Norwegian (Ingebrigtsen model):** 4-5 double-threshold days/week, 160-180 km/week, lactate monitoring at every session.
- **Adapted Norwegian (recreational):** 2-3 threshold sessions/week (not doubles), 60-100 km/week, pace-based rather than lactate-based.
- **Norwegian-Polarized hybrid:** 3 threshold + 1 VO2max per week, easier days strictly Zone 1. Attempts to combine threshold volume with polarized recovery.
- **Marathon Norwegian:** Threshold sessions extend to 20-40 min continuous at marathon pace/LT1. Fewer intervals, more continuous threshold work.

## 5. Block Periodization (Issurin/Verkhoshansky)

**Domain:** All strength/power/speed sports, also applied to endurance
**Primary Source:** "Block Periodization" (Vladimir Issurin, 2008, 2019), "Supertraining" (Verkhoshansky & Siff), "Special Strength Training Manual for Coaches" (Verkhoshansky)

### 5.1 Intensity Distribution

Varies dramatically by block:

| Block | % HIGH (>85%) | % MEDIUM (70-85%) | % LOW (<70%) |
|-------|--------------|-------------------|-------------|
| Accumulation | 10-20% | 30-40% | 40-60% |
| Transmutation | 30-50% | 30-40% | 20-30% |
| Realization | 50-70% | 20-30% | 10-20% |

**Critical:** The intensity distribution SHIFTS dramatically between blocks. In any given 2-4 week block, one quality dominates. This is the opposite of DUP (which varies within each week) and different from linear periodization (which shifts gradually over months).

### 5.2 Session Type Mix

**Accumulation Block (2-4 weeks):**
- 3-5 sessions/week dominated by HIGH VOLUME, MODERATE INTENSITY
- Hypertrophy: 3-5 sets x 8-12 reps at 65-78% 1RM
- Aerobic base work, general physical preparation
- 1-2 maintenance sessions of other qualities

**Transmutation Block (2-4 weeks):**
- 3-5 sessions/week dominated by MODERATE VOLUME, HIGH INTENSITY
- Strength: 4-6 sets x 3-6 reps at 80-90% 1RM
- Sport-specific power development
- 1 maintenance session of aerobic capacity

**Realization Block (1-2 weeks):**
- 2-4 sessions/week dominated by LOW VOLUME, VERY HIGH INTENSITY
- Peak performance: 1-3 reps at 90-100%+ 1RM
- Speed/power expression, competition simulation
- Significant volume reduction

### 5.3 Volume-Intensity Relationship

**INVERSE ACROSS BLOCKS (sequential):**
- Accumulation: HIGH volume, LOW-MEDIUM intensity
- Transmutation: MEDIUM volume, MEDIUM-HIGH intensity
- Realization: LOW volume, HIGH intensity

Within each block, volume and intensity are relatively stable (NOT undulating). The shift happens at block transitions, not within blocks.

**Key metric:** Volume drops ~30-40% from Accumulation to Transmutation, and another ~30-40% from Transmutation to Realization.

### 5.4 Deload Pattern

- **Built into structure:** The Realization block IS the deload/taper
- **Between A-T-R cycles:** Brief transition (3-5 days) between cycles
- **Within blocks:** Typically NO deload within a 2-4 week block (it's too short to need one)
- **Unique:** The block structure itself handles recovery through sequential loading of different qualities -- when you move to Transmutation (strength), the volume overload from Accumulation dissipates

### 5.5 Progression Model

**CONCENTRATED SEQUENTIAL LOADING:**
- Each block focuses on 1-2 "target abilities" (the concentrated load)
- Other abilities are maintained at minimal effective dose
- Progression happens BETWEEN A-T-R cycles: each subsequent Accumulation block starts at a higher baseline
- Residual training effects: aerobic capacity (lasts 25-30 days after block), strength (lasts 25-35 days), speed (lasts 5-8 days), power (lasts 10-15 days)
- Block sequencing is designed so that the quality trained most recently has the shortest residual and is closest to competition

### 5.6 Recovery Spacing

- **Within Accumulation:** 24-48 hours between sessions (moderate intensity allows faster recovery)
- **Within Transmutation:** 48-72 hours between high-intensity sessions
- **Within Realization:** 72-96 hours between maximal sessions
- **Between blocks:** 2-5 transition days (active recovery)

### 5.7 Unique Discriminatory Markers

1. **A-T-R sequence** (Accumulation-Transmutation-Realization) -- This specific 3-block sequence is unique to block periodization
2. **2-4 week blocks** -- Mesocycles are shorter than linear periodization (which uses 4-6 week mesocycles)
3. **One dominant training quality per block** -- NOT multiple qualities per week (which is DUP) and NOT gradual shifts (which is linear)
4. **Maintenance sessions for non-targeted qualities** -- 1-2 sessions/week at minimal dose to preserve previous block's gains
5. **Residual training effect awareness** -- Block sequencing considers how long each quality persists after directed training stops
6. **Dramatic shifts at block boundaries** -- Week 4 might be 80% hypertrophy, Week 5 is 80% strength. This abrupt shift is distinctive.

### 5.8 Hard Exclusion Rules

- **DISQUALIFIED if:** All training qualities are trained equally in every week (that's DUP or concurrent)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Blocks are longer than 6 weeks (block periodization uses SHORT concentrated blocks)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Volume and intensity change gradually week-by-week (that's linear periodization)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Rep ranges stay constant across the entire program
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** There's no identifiable shift in dominant training quality between phases
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** A single block trains more than 3 primary qualities simultaneously
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** The sequence is NOT organized with volume-first, intensity-later logic

### 5.9 Typical Block/Phase Structure

| Block | Duration | Primary Quality | Volume | Intensity | Rep Range |
|-------|----------|----------------|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Accumulation 1 | 2-4 weeks | Hypertrophy / Work Capacity | HIGH | LOW-MODERATE | 8-15 reps at 60-75% |
| Transmutation 1 | 2-4 weeks | Max Strength | MODERATE | HIGH | 3-6 reps at 80-92% |
| Realization 1 | 1-2 weeks | Power / Speed / Competition | LOW | VERY HIGH | 1-3 reps at 90-100%+ |
| Transition | 3-5 days | Recovery | MINIMAL | LOW | Unstructured |
| Accumulation 2 | 2-4 weeks | (Repeat at higher level) | HIGH | LOW-MODERATE | Same ranges, more volume |

**Multiple A-T-R cycles per macrocycle:** 2-4 cycles in a competition season

### 5.10 Common Variations

- **Pure Issurin Block:** Strict A-T-R, 2-3 week blocks, one quality per block, minimal maintenance of others.
- **Modified Block (Bompa-style):** Longer blocks (3-5 weeks), more gradual transitions. Blurs toward linear periodization.
- **Conjugate Block (Bondarchuk):** Multiple qualities trained simultaneously but with rotating emphasis. Different from Issurin's concentrated loading.
- **Endurance Block Periodization:** Accumulation = aerobic base, Transmutation = threshold/VO2max, Realization = race-specific + taper.
- **Team Sport Block:** Blocks aligned with competitive calendar; in-season blocks are shortened to 2-3 weeks with maintenance focus.

## 6. Linear Periodization (Matveyev)

**Domain:** All sports (originally developed for Olympic sports in Soviet system)
**Primary Source:** "Fundamentals of Sports Training" (Lev Matveyev, 1981), "Periodization: Theory and Methodology of Training" (Tudor Bompa)

### 6.1 Intensity Distribution

Changes gradually across the macrocycle:

| Mesocycle | % HIGH (>85%) | % MEDIUM (70-85%) | % LOW (<70%) |
|-----------|--------------|-------------------|-------------|
| Hypertrophy (weeks 1-4) | 0-5% | 20-30% | 65-80% |
| Strength (weeks 5-8) | 15-25% | 40-50% | 25-35% |
| Power (weeks 9-12) | 30-45% | 30-40% | 15-25% |
| Peaking (weeks 13-16) | 50-65% | 25-35% | 10-20% |

### 6.2 Session Type Mix (varies by phase)

**Hypertrophy Phase (4-6 weeks):**
- 4-5 sessions/week, all following hypertrophy parameters
- 3-5 sets x 8-12 reps at 60-75% 1RM
- 60-90 seconds rest between sets
- General exercises dominate

**Basic Strength Phase (4-6 weeks):**
- 4-5 sessions/week
- 4-6 sets x 4-6 reps at 78-85% 1RM
- 2-3 minutes rest
- Mix of general and specific exercises

**Strength-Power Phase (3-4 weeks):**
- 3-4 sessions/week
- 3-5 sets x 2-4 reps at 85-93% 1RM + explosive work
- 3-5 minutes rest
- Specific exercises dominate

**Peaking Phase (2-3 weeks):**
- 2-3 sessions/week
- 1-3 sets x 1-3 reps at 93-100% 1RM
- 5+ minutes rest
- Competition-specific only

### 6.3 Volume-Intensity Relationship

**CLASSIC INVERSE (the textbook model):**
- Week 1: Volume = HIGH (e.g., 20-25 total sets/session), Intensity = LOW (60-70%)
- Week 16: Volume = LOW (e.g., 6-10 total sets/session), Intensity = HIGH (93-100%)
- The lines CROSS somewhere around weeks 8-10

This is the most straightforward inverse relationship of any methodology. Volume decreases monotonically while intensity increases monotonically. There are no waves or undulations.

### 6.4 Deload Pattern

- **Frequency:** End of each mesocycle (every 4-6 weeks)
- **Volume reduction:** 30-50% volume reduction
- **Intensity:** Maintained or slightly reduced (5-10%)
- **Duration:** 1 week (the 4th or 5th week of each mesocycle)
- **Standard pattern:** 3:1 or 4:1 (3-4 loading weeks : 1 deload week)
- **Deload is formalized and systematic** -- unlike Lydiard (no deloads) or Block (deload built into realization)

### 6.5 Progression Model

**UNIDIRECTIONAL LINEAR:**
- Within each mesocycle: 2-3% increase in training load per week
- Across mesocycles: rep ranges decrease while % 1RM increases
- Hypertrophy -> Strength -> Power -> Peaking: each phase uses heavier loads and fewer reps
- No returning to previous phase parameters (unidirectional)
- Load increases are percentage-based and predictable

### 6.6 Recovery Spacing

- **Hypertrophy phase:** 24-48 hours between sessions for same muscle group (higher frequency, lower intensity)
- **Strength phase:** 48-72 hours between heavy sessions
- **Power/Peaking phase:** 72-96 hours between maximal sessions
- **Recovery spacing increases as intensity increases** -- this is a gradient, not a fixed rule

### 6.7 Unique Discriminatory Markers

1. **Monotonic decrease in reps + monotonic increase in %1RM across the entire program** -- No other method shows this perfectly linear trend
2. **Phase names follow: Hypertrophy -> Strength -> Power -> Peaking** -- This specific 4-phase sequence
3. **Each phase uses ONE rep range** -- Phase 1 is 8-12, Phase 2 is 4-6, Phase 3 is 2-4, Phase 4 is 1-3. They don't overlap significantly.
4. **No returning to previous rep ranges** -- Once you leave 8-12 reps (hypertrophy), you never go back during that macrocycle
5. **4-6 week mesocycles** -- Longer than block periodization (2-4 weeks) but similar to general periodization
6. **General-to-specific exercise progression** -- Early phases use more general exercises, later phases use competition-specific movements

### 6.8 Hard Exclusion Rules

- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Multiple rep ranges appear in the same week (e.g., 12 reps AND 3 reps in same week = not linear)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Rep ranges increase at any point in the program (going from 5 reps back to 10 reps)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Blocks are shorter than 3 weeks (linear uses 4-6 week phases)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Intensity decreases at any point (except deload weeks)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Volume increases in the second half of the program
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** The program cycles back to high-volume phases after peaking (that's undulating or repeated blocks)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Multiple training qualities are emphasized simultaneously within a week

### 6.9 Typical Block/Phase Structure

| Phase | Duration | Sets x Reps | Intensity | Volume (sets/session) | Focus |
|-------|----------|------------|-----------|----------------------|-------|
| Anatomical Adaptation | 2-4 weeks | 2-3 x 12-15 | 50-65% | 15-20 sets | Movement patterns, conditioning |
| Hypertrophy | 4-6 weeks | 3-5 x 8-12 | 65-78% | 16-25 sets | Muscle cross-section |
| Basic Strength | 4-6 weeks | 4-6 x 4-6 | 78-88% | 12-18 sets | Neural drive, max strength |
| Strength-Power | 3-4 weeks | 3-5 x 2-4 | 85-93% | 9-15 sets | Rate of force development |
| Peaking/Competition | 2-3 weeks | 1-3 x 1-3 | 93-100%+ | 5-9 sets | Competition performance |
| Transition/Active Rest | 2-4 weeks | Unstructured | Low | Minimal | Recovery |

**Total macrocycle:** 16-24 weeks (single or double periodization per year)

### 6.10 Common Variations

- **Pure Matveyev Linear:** Strictly unidirectional, one rep range per phase, no variation within phases.
- **Bompa Linear:** Adds anatomical adaptation phase at start. Slightly more flexible phase durations.
- **Reverse Linear:** Starts with high intensity/low volume, progresses to low intensity/high volume. Used for hypertrophy endpoints instead of strength endpoints.
- **Modified Linear (weekly undulation within phases):** Each phase has a heavy/medium/light day within the week, but rep ranges still progress linearly across phases. This hybrid is very common.
- **Double/Triple Periodization:** 2-3 macrocycles per year (for sports with multiple competition peaks). Each macrocycle is a compressed linear progression.

## 7. Daily Undulating Periodization (DUP)

**Domain:** Strength Training / Powerlifting / General Fitness
**Primary Source:** Research by Mike Zourdos, Brad Schoenfeld, Greg Nuckols. "Daily Undulating Periodization: A Systematic Review" (Harries et al., 2015). Originally described by Poliquin and Kraemer.

### 7.1 Intensity Distribution

Within ANY given week:

| Day Type | Intensity | Sets x Reps |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| Hypertrophy Day | 65-75% 1RM | 3-4 x 8-12 |
| Strength Day | 80-88% 1RM | 4-5 x 3-5 |
| Power Day | 70-80% 1RM (explosive) or 88-95% (heavy singles/doubles) | 3-5 x 1-3 or 5-8 x 2-3 |

**Weekly distribution:** ~33% each (hypertrophy, strength, power) -- though some programs weight strength more heavily.

### 7.2 Session Type Mix (Typical Week)

**Classic 3-day DUP:**

| Day | Focus | Example |
|-----|-------|---------|
| Monday | Hypertrophy | Squat 4x10@70%, Bench 4x10@70%, Row 4x10 |
| Wednesday | Strength | Squat 5x5@82%, Bench 5x5@82%, Deadlift 5x3@85% |
| Friday | Power | Squat 5x3@88%, Bench 5x2@90%, Clean 5x2@80% |

**Classic 4-day DUP:**

| Day | Focus | Example |
|-----|-------|---------|
| Monday | Hypertrophy (Upper) | Bench 4x10@70%, OHP 3x10@68%, Rows 4x10 |
| Tuesday | Hypertrophy (Lower) | Squat 4x10@70%, RDL 3x10@68%, Lunges 3x10 |
| Thursday | Strength (Upper) | Bench 5x5@82%, OHP 4x4@80%, Rows 5x5 |
| Friday | Strength (Lower) | Squat 5x5@82%, Deadlift 4x3@85%, Front Squat 3x5 |

**Frequency per lift:** Each main lift hit 2-3x per week at different intensities/rep ranges

### 7.3 Volume-Intensity Relationship

**DAILY INVERSE (unique pattern):**
- Within each session: standard inverse (more reps = lower weight)
- Across the week: UNDULATING -- high-volume Monday, high-intensity Friday
- Across the mesocycle: Gradual increase in all parameters (not a large shift)
- The KEY pattern: volume and intensity change DAILY, not weekly or monthly

Volume and intensity both undulate day-to-day but with overall weekly averages remaining relatively stable or gradually progressing.

### 7.4 Deload Pattern

- **Frequency:** Every 4-6 weeks
- **Volume reduction:** 40-50% across all session types
- **Intensity reduction:** 5-10% on all days
- **Unique:** Deload maintains the undulating structure -- still a hypertrophy day, strength day, power day, but all at reduced loads
- **Some DUP programs autoregulate deloads** via RPE/RIR (if RPE exceeds targets for 2+ sessions, deload)

### 7.5 Progression Model

**MULTI-VARIABLE MICRO-PROGRESSION:**
- Each day type progresses independently:
  - Hypertrophy: Add 1-2 reps per session, or add 2.5-5 lbs when rep target is hit
  - Strength: Add 2.5-5 lbs per session when all sets completed at target reps
  - Power: Increase by 2.5-5 lbs every 1-2 weeks
- Weekly overload: Total weekly volume load (sets x reps x weight) increases 2-5%
- After deload: Resume at pre-deload weights or slight increase
- Progression is CONTINUOUS and SMALL -- not phase-based jumps

### 7.6 Recovery Spacing

- **Between sessions of same type:** N/A (each day is different)
- **Between sessions for same muscle group:** 48-72 hours (if doing full-body DUP)
- **Rest days:** 1-3 per week (commonly trained 3-4 days/week)
- **Key insight:** Because intensity varies daily, CNS fatigue is distributed -- heavy day is followed by lighter day naturally

### 7.7 Unique Discriminatory Markers

1. **Same lifts trained at different rep ranges within the same week** -- This is THE defining feature. Squats at 10 reps Monday, 5 reps Wednesday, 3 reps Friday.
2. **No distinct multi-week phases** -- The same structure repeats weekly, not changing phase every 4-6 weeks
3. **Rep range diversity within every week** -- Every week contains hypertrophy (8-12), strength (3-5), AND power (1-3) rep ranges
4. **Same exercise appearing 2-3x per week at different parameters** -- Unlike body-part splits where each muscle is hit 1x/week
5. **No progressive narrowing of rep ranges over time** -- Rep ranges stay diverse throughout the entire program
6. **Total weekly volume relatively stable** -- Not decreasing over time like linear periodization

### 7.8 Hard Exclusion Rules

- **DISQUALIFIED if:** All sessions in a week use the same rep range (that's linear or block)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Rep ranges change only between multi-week phases (that's linear periodization)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Volume decreases monotonically over the program (that's linear)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Only 1 session per week per major lift (DUP requires 2-3)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Training is organized by body parts rather than movement patterns (DUP is typically full-body or upper/lower)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** A clear multi-week block focuses on one quality (that's block periodization)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Exercise selection changes dramatically between sessions (same lifts, different parameters is the DUP hallmark)

### 7.9 Typical Block/Phase Structure

DUP is MINIMALLY PERIODIZED by design:

| Period | Duration | Structure | Weekly Pattern |
|--------|----------|-----------|---------------|
| Introductory | 1-2 weeks | Conservative loads | Hypertrophy (10s) / Strength (5s) / Power (3s) -- all at reduced intensity |
| Accumulation | 4-6 weeks | Progressive overload | Same H/S/P split, loads increase session-to-session |
| Deload | 1 week | Volume/intensity reduction | Same H/S/P split at 50-60% of recent loads |
| Intensification | 4-6 weeks | Higher baseline loads | Same H/S/P split, heavier starting point |
| Competition Prep (optional) | 2-3 weeks | Reduced volume | Power day becomes primary, H day becomes lighter |

**Key:** The weekly H/S/P structure is maintained across all periods. There's no "switch" to a different training paradigm.

### 7.10 Common Variations

- **Pure DUP (3-day):** Mon Hypertrophy, Wed Strength, Fri Power. Full body each day.
- **4-day Upper/Lower DUP:** Alternating upper/lower with hypertrophy/strength rotation.
- **Modified DUP (Zourdos):** Hypertrophy day uses RPE 6-7, Strength day RPE 8-9, Power day RPE 7-8. Autoregulated.
- **Weekly Undulating Periodization (WUP):** Each WEEK has a focus (week 1 hypertrophy, week 2 strength, week 3 power), but within each week all sessions are the same type. Some call this "weekly undulating" as distinct from "daily undulating."
- **DUP + Accessories:** Main lifts follow DUP, but accessory work follows bodybuilding (fixed rep range) protocols.
- **Powerlifting DUP:** Hypertrophy day uses competition lifts with pauses, Strength day uses variations (close-grip bench, front squat), Power day uses competition lifts at competition intensity.

## 8. Westside Barbell Conjugate (Simmons)

**Domain:** Powerlifting / Strength Sports
**Primary Source:** "Westside Barbell Book of Methods" (Louie Simmons, 2007), "Special Strengths Development for All Sports" (Simmons), Westside Barbell articles and seminars

### 8.1 Intensity Distribution

| Day Type | % of Sessions | Intensity |
|----------|--------------|-----------|
| Max Effort (ME) | 25% (2 sessions/week) | 90-100%+ 1RM (work up to 1-3RM) |
| Dynamic Effort (DE) | 25% (2 sessions/week) | 50-60% 1RM + 25-30% band/chain tension (total ~75-85%) |
| Repetition Effort (RE/accessories) | 50% (within ME and DE sessions) | 60-75% 1RM (hypertrophy/weak point work) |

### 8.2 Session Type Mix (Typical Week)

| Day | Type | Structure |
|-----|------|-----------|
| Monday | MAX EFFORT Upper | Work up to 1-3RM on a pressing variation (floor press, board press, incline, etc.), then 3-4 RE accessory exercises for triceps, lats, shoulders (3-5 sets x 6-15 reps) |
| Wednesday | MAX EFFORT Lower | Work up to 1-3RM on a squat/deadlift variation (box squat, rack pull, SSB squat, etc.), then 3-4 RE accessories for hamstrings, glutes, back (3-5 sets x 6-15 reps) |
| Friday | DYNAMIC EFFORT Upper | Bench press 8-9 sets x 3 reps at 50-60% + bands/chains (30-45 sec rest), then 3-4 RE accessories |
| Sunday | DYNAMIC EFFORT Lower | Box squat 10-12 sets x 2 reps at 50-60% + bands/chains (45-60 sec rest), then 3-4 RE accessories |

**Training frequency:** 4 sessions/week (fixed, rarely varies)
**Session duration:** 60-90 minutes including accessories

### 8.3 Volume-Intensity Relationship

**CONCURRENT with fixed structure:**
- Max Effort provides HIGH intensity, LOW volume (1-3 reps at max)
- Dynamic Effort provides MODERATE intensity, MODERATE-HIGH volume (20-27 total reps of main lift per session)
- Repetition Effort provides LOW-MODERATE intensity, HIGH volume (accessories total 100-200 reps)
- ALL three methods trained EVERY week -- no sequential phasing
- Weekly total volume is relatively CONSTANT (doesn't change much week to week)

### 8.4 Deload Pattern

- **No traditional deload weeks** in classic Westside programming
- **Recovery is managed via exercise rotation:** switching to a new ME exercise every 1-3 weeks prevents staleness without reducing load
- **DE waves:** Dynamic effort percentages wave in 3-week cycles:
  - Week 1: 50% + bands
  - Week 2: 55% + bands
  - Week 3: 60% + bands
  - Then reset to 50% with potentially heavier bands (this functions as a micro-deload)
- **ME exercise rotation IS the deload mechanism** -- by changing exercises, you reset the intensity curve without taking a light week

### 8.5 Progression Model

**CONJUGATE ROTATION (not linear):**
- ME exercises rotate every 1-3 weeks to prevent accommodation
- Goal: set a new 1-3RM record on each exercise variation each time it appears in rotation
- DE percentages follow 3-week wave: 50% -> 55% -> 60% (of competition max) + accommodating resistance
- Accessory (RE) work progresses via adding reps, then adding weight when rep target is exceeded
- Long-term progression tracked by competition PR, not training maxes

### 8.6 Recovery Spacing

- **Between ME sessions:** 72 hours (Mon upper ME -> Wed lower ME -> Fri upper DE -> Sun lower DE)
- **Same movement pattern heavy loading:** 72-96 hours between heavy upper pressing (ME Mon) and speed upper pressing (DE Fri)
- **Between sessions:** Always 1-2 rest days between each training day
- **72-hour rotation** between max effort and dynamic effort for same body region

### 8.7 Unique Discriminatory Markers

1. **Max Effort + Dynamic Effort dual system** -- No other method pairs maxing out (1-3RM) with speed work (50-60% + bands) in the same weekly plan
2. **Accommodating resistance (bands and chains)** -- Pervasive use, present in EVERY DE session. Key identifier.
3. **Exercise rotation every 1-3 weeks** on ME days -- The exercise itself changes, not just the weight/reps
4. **Box squats as a staple** -- Box squat appears in nearly every Westside program (DE Lower day)
5. **8-12 sets of 2-3 reps on DE days** -- This specific set/rep scheme with short rest (30-60 sec) is unique
6. **Exactly 4 training days/week:** ME Upper, ME Lower, DE Upper, DE Lower
7. **No sequential phases** -- All qualities (max strength, speed-strength, hypertrophy via accessories) trained every week, year-round
8. **Weak point training via accessories** -- Accessories are selected to address specific sticking points, not general bodybuilding

### 8.8 Hard Exclusion Rules

- **DISQUALIFIED if:** No sessions involve working up to 1-3RM (max effort is non-negotiable)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** No speed/explosive work at sub-maximal loads with compensatory acceleration
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Training has distinct multi-week phases (hypertrophy phase -> strength phase = NOT conjugate)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Exercises stay the same for more than 3 weeks on max effort days
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** No mention/use of bands, chains, or accommodating resistance
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Program follows a body-part split (chest day, back day, etc.)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Training is fewer than 4 or more than 5 days per week
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Rep ranges exceed 5 on main lifts (except accessories)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** DE days use heavy loads (>70% bar weight) without accommodating resistance

### 8.9 Typical Block/Phase Structure

Westside is NOT periodized in the traditional sense:

| Component | Structure | Duration | Notes |
|-----------|-----------|----------|-------|
| ME Upper | Rotate exercise every 1-3 weeks | Ongoing | Floor press -> Board press -> Close grip -> ... |
| ME Lower | Rotate exercise every 1-3 weeks | Ongoing | Box squat -> Rack pull -> GM -> ... |
| DE Upper | 3-week wave (50/55/60% + AR) | Repeating | Band tension may change between waves |
| DE Lower | 3-week wave (50/55/60% + AR) | Repeating | Box height/stance may vary between waves |
| Competition Prep | 4-6 weeks pre-meet | Periodic | ME exercises become closer to competition lifts, DE intensity may increase |

**Key:** There is NO off-season/in-season distinction. The system runs year-round with the same structure, only tweaking exercise selection and accommodating resistance levels.

### 8.10 Common Variations

- **Pure Westside:** 4-day split exactly as Simmons prescribed. ME rotation, DE with bands/chains, box squats always.
- **Conjugate for Athletes (non-powerlifters):** Adds sport-specific conditioning on off days. DE work may include Olympic lifts or jumps instead of barbell speed work.
- **Conjugate without accommodating resistance:** Some gyms don't have bands/chains, so they use straight weight for DE. Loses a key discriminatory marker.
- **Concurrent/Conjugate hybrid:** Adds a 5th "repetition effort" day for hypertrophy. Popular with raw lifters who need more muscle mass.
- **5/3/1-Conjugate hybrid:** Uses 5/3/1 percentages for main work but Westside accessories and exercise rotation.

## 9. 5/3/1 (Wendler)

**Domain:** Strength Training / Powerlifting / General Strength
**Primary Source:** "5/3/1: The Simplest and Most Effective Training System for Raw Strength" (Jim Wendler, 2011), "5/3/1 Forever" (Wendler, 2017), "Beyond 5/3/1" (Wendler)

### 9.1 Intensity Distribution

Based on TRAINING MAX (TM = 85-90% of true 1RM):

| Week | Main Work Sets (% of TM) | Actual % of True 1RM |
|------|-------------------------|---------------------|
| Week 1 (5s week) | 65% x 5, 75% x 5, 85% x 5+ | 55%, 64%, 72% of true 1RM |
| Week 2 (3s week) | 70% x 3, 80% x 3, 85% x 3+ | 60%, 68%, 72% of true 1RM |
| Week 3 (1s week) | 75% x 5, 85% x 3, 95% x 1+ | 64%, 72%, 81% of true 1RM |
| Week 4 (Deload) | 40% x 5, 50% x 5, 60% x 5 | 34%, 43%, 51% of true 1RM |

**Critical:** The actual intensities relative to true 1RM are MODERATE -- the heaviest set in the entire cycle is only ~81% of true 1RM (95% of a 85% training max). This is MUCH lower than Westside ME days (95-100%+ of true 1RM).

### 9.2 Session Type Mix (Typical Week)

| Day | Main Lift | Supplemental | Assistance |
|-----|-----------|-------------|------------|
| Monday | Squat (5/3/1 sets) | 5x5 FSL (First Set Last = 65-75% TM) | Push + Pull + Single-leg/Core (25-50 reps each) |
| Tuesday | Bench Press (5/3/1 sets) | 5x5 FSL | Push + Pull + Single-leg/Core |
| Thursday | Deadlift (5/3/1 sets) | 5x5 FSL | Push + Pull + Single-leg/Core |
| Friday | OHP (5/3/1 sets) | 5x5 FSL | Push + Pull + Single-leg/Core |

**Frequency:** 4 days/week (sometimes 3 with condensed schedule)
**Each main lift:** 1x per week (very low frequency per lift compared to DUP's 2-3x)

### 9.3 Volume-Intensity Relationship

**MICRO-UNDULATING (3-week wave):**
- Week 1: Moderate volume (5s), moderate intensity (85% TM top set)
- Week 2: Lower volume (3s), higher intensity (85% TM top set, fewer reps prescribed)
- Week 3: Lowest volume (1+), highest intensity (95% TM top set)
- Week 4: Lowest everything (deload)

The relationship is a MILD inverse within each 3-week cycle. But the range is narrow -- intensity only spans 65-95% of TM (55-81% of true 1RM). Supplemental volume stays relatively constant.

### 9.4 Deload Pattern

- **Frequency:** Every 4th week (strictly 3:1 loading cycle) -- this is non-negotiable in 5/3/1
- **Volume reduction:** Main work drops to 3 sets of 5 at 40/50/60% TM
- **"7th Week Protocol" (from 5/3/1 Forever):** Deload every 7th week instead (two 3-week cycles + 1 deload). TM test singles at 100% TM to verify TM accuracy.
- **Deload is MANDATORY** -- Wendler explicitly says never skip deload weeks

### 9.5 Progression Model

**SLOW LINEAR micro-loading:**
- After each 3-week cycle (or 7th week protocol): add 5 lbs to upper body TM, 10 lbs to lower body TM
- This is ~2.5-5 kg per cycle (monthly progression)
- Annual progression rate: 30-60 lbs on upper lifts, 60-120 lbs on lower lifts (in theory)
- If the AMRAP set on 95% week drops below 3-5 reps, reduce TM by 10%
- **Training max concept:** Progress is deliberately slow. Start light, progress slow, set PRs on AMRAP sets. This ultra-conservative loading is a strong discriminator.

### 9.6 Recovery Spacing

- **Between sessions:** 1-2 rest days (4 sessions in 7 days)
- **Each main lift hit 1x/week** -- maximum recovery between same-lift sessions
- **No daily undulation stress** -- each day is a different lift, so recovery is managed by body part separation
- **Conditioning on off-days:** 2-3 conditioning sessions per week (pushing sled, hill sprints, prowler) -- Wendler strongly advocates for conditioning

### 9.7 Unique Discriminatory Markers

1. **The 5/3/1 rep scheme:** Week 1 = sets of 5, Week 2 = sets of 3, Week 3 = 5/3/1 (ascending intensity). This specific weekly rep pattern is unique.
2. **Training Max concept:** Working off 85-90% of true 1RM, not actual 1RM. Top set is ~81% of true max.
3. **AMRAP "plus" sets:** The final set each day is an "as many reps as possible" set (denoted 5+, 3+, 1+). This is a strong identifier.
4. **65/75/85 -> 70/80/85 -> 75/85/95 percentage progression** (of TM) -- This exact sequence is unique to 5/3/1.
5. **Mandatory deload every 3rd or 7th week** -- More structured than any other method's deload protocol
6. **+5 lbs upper / +10 lbs lower per cycle** -- This specific increment is prescribed and distinctive
7. **Four lifts only as main movements:** Squat, Bench, Deadlift, OHP. Each trained 1x/week. No exercise rotation (unlike Westside).
8. **FSL (First Set Last) supplemental work** -- Supplemental volume at Week's first set weight (65-75% TM)
9. **Push/Pull/Legs-Core assistance template** -- 25-50 reps each category per session

### 9.8 Hard Exclusion Rules

- **DISQUALIFIED if:** True 1RM percentages exceed 85% regularly (5/3/1 tops at ~81% of true max)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** No AMRAP/plus sets present (every 5/3/1 program has them)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Main lifts are trained more than 1x per week (DUP trains them 2-3x)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Exercise selection rotates frequently (5/3/1 uses the same 4 lifts throughout)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** No deload weeks present in the program
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Progression exceeds 10 lbs per cycle on any lift (5/3/1 is deliberately slow)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Program has distinct hypertrophy/strength/peaking phases (5/3/1 is steady-state)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Training is more than 4 days per week on the main program
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Bands, chains, or accommodating resistance are central to the program (that's Westside)

### 9.9 Typical Block/Phase Structure

5/3/1 is **NOT periodized in blocks:**

| Cycle | Duration | Structure | TM Change |
|-------|----------|-----------|-----------|
| Cycle 1 | 4 weeks | Week 1 (5s) -> Week 2 (3s) -> Week 3 (1s) -> Deload | Starting TM |
| Cycle 2 | 4 weeks | Same structure | +5 upper, +10 lower |
| Cycle 3 | 4 weeks | Same structure | +5 upper, +10 lower |
| ... | ... | Same structure repeating | Continuous slow increase |
| TM Reset | When needed | Drop TM by 10-15% | Reset and rebuild |

**Leaders and Anchors (5/3/1 Forever):**
- Leader cycles (2-3 cycles): Higher supplemental volume (BBB 5x10, BBS 10x5), less intensity
- Anchor cycles (1-2 cycles): Less supplemental volume, PR sets emphasized, more intense
- This Leader/Anchor system is the closest 5/3/1 comes to periodization

### 9.10 Common Variations

- **Original 5/3/1:** Just the main 5/3/1 sets + AMRAP. Minimal supplemental.
- **BBB (Boring But Big):** 5/3/1 main sets + 5x10 at 50-70% TM of same lift. High hypertrophy volume.
- **FSL (First Set Last):** 5/3/1 main sets + 5x5 at week's first working set percentage.
- **BBS (Boring But Strong):** 5/3/1 main sets + 10x5 at FSL weight. Volume-focused.
- **SSL (Second Set Last):** 5/3/1 main + supplemental at second set weight.
- **Triumvirate:** 5/3/1 + two assistance exercises only (3 exercises total per day).
- **5/3/1 for Beginners:** 2 main lifts per day, 3 days per week. Faster progression.
- **Coffinworm, Leviathan, God is a Beast:** Advanced templates from "5/3/1 Forever" with specific Leader/Anchor combinations.

## 10. Triphasic Training (Dietz)

**Domain:** Strength & Power Development for Athletes (especially collegiate football, hockey, basketball)
**Primary Source:** "Triphasic Training: A Systematic Approach to Elite Speed and Explosive Strength Performance" (Cal Dietz & Ben Peterson, 2012)

### 10.1 Intensity Distribution

| Phase | % HIGH (>85%) | % MEDIUM (70-85%) | % LOW (<70%) |
|-------|--------------|-------------------|-------------|
| Eccentric (Block 1) | 40-60% | 30-40% | 10-20% |
| Isometric (Block 2) | 40-60% | 30-40% | 10-20% |
| Concentric/Reactive (Block 3) | 50-70% | 20-30% | 10-20% |

**Note:** Intensity is consistently moderate-to-high across all phases. What changes is the CONTRACTION TYPE, not just the load. This is the key distinction.

### 10.2 Session Type Mix (Typical Week - per phase)

**Eccentric Phase (2-3 weeks):**

| Day | Session |
|-----|---------|
| Monday | Eccentric squat: 3-5 sets x 2-4 reps, 5-6 second eccentric lowering at 80-90% 1RM + 0-20% overshoot (drop/release at bottom) |
| Tuesday | Upper body eccentric: Bench 3-5 x 2-4, 5-6 sec eccentric at 80-90%, plyometric work (reactive) |
| Thursday | Eccentric deadlift/hinge: 3-5 x 2-4, 5-6 sec eccentric, sport-specific conditioning |
| Friday | Upper body eccentric + accessory work |

**Isometric Phase (2-3 weeks):**

| Day | Session |
|-----|---------|
| Monday | Isometric squat: 3-5 sets x 2-4 reps with 3-6 second PAUSE at sticking point, 80-90% 1RM |
| Tuesday | Upper body iso: Bench with 3-6 sec pause at chest, plyometric work |
| Thursday | Isometric hinge: Deadlift with pause at knee, 80-90% |
| Friday | Upper iso + accessories |

**Concentric/Reactive Phase (2-3 weeks):**

| Day | Session |
|-----|---------|
| Monday | Concentric emphasis squat: 3-5 x 1-3 reps, EXPLOSIVE concentric from dead stop, 85-95% |
| Tuesday | Upper body concentric: Bench from pins, explosive press, plyometrics |
| Thursday | Concentric pulls: Block pulls, explosive deadlift from pins |
| Friday | Sport-specific power expression, reactive training |

### 10.3 Volume-Intensity Relationship

**STABLE INTENSITY, SHIFTING CONTRACTION TYPE:**
- Intensity stays in the 80-95% range across ALL three phases
- Volume is moderate (2-4 reps, 3-5 sets) across ALL three phases
- What shifts is the TEMPO and CONTRACTION TYPE (eccentric emphasis -> isometric emphasis -> concentric emphasis)
- This is fundamentally different from linear periodization (where intensity increases and volume decreases) or block periodization (where the quality shifts)

### 10.4 Deload Pattern

- **Between phases:** 2-3 transition days between each triphasic block
- **Within phases:** Phases are only 2-3 weeks, so no deload within them
- **After completing the full triphasic cycle (6-9 weeks):** 1 week deload/unloading
- **The phase transition itself functions as a partial deload** -- switching contraction types gives specific structures recovery while maintaining intensity

### 10.5 Progression Model

**CONTRACTION-TYPE SEQUENTIAL:**
1. Eccentric: Build eccentric strength and force absorption capacity (slow eccentrics at supramaximal loads possible with eccentric overload)
2. Isometric: Develop rate of force development at the sticking point (pauses at the weakest ROM position)
3. Concentric: Express power with explosive concentric from dead stop or reactive positions
4. Each phase builds on the previous: eccentric strength -> isometric RFD -> concentric power expression

Within each phase: load may increase 2-5% per week or reps may increase from 2 to 4.

### 10.6 Recovery Spacing

- **Between sessions:** 48-72 hours for same movement pattern
- **Eccentric phase demands MORE recovery:** DOMS is severe with slow eccentrics, so 72 hours minimum between heavy eccentric sessions for same muscles
- **Isometric phase:** 48-72 hours between sessions
- **Concentric phase:** 48 hours sufficient (less muscle damage from concentric-only work)
- **Training frequency:** 3-4 days/week

### 10.7 Unique Discriminatory Markers

1. **THREE distinct contraction-type phases: Eccentric -> Isometric -> Concentric** -- NO other method structures phases by contraction type
2. **Prescribed TEMPO with specific eccentric durations** -- "5-6 second eccentric" is explicit in programming. Other methods don't prescribe 5-6 second eccentrics as the primary training variable.
3. **Isometric PAUSES at sticking points** -- 3-6 second pauses at specific ROM positions, not just regular pause reps
4. **Dead-stop concentric work** -- Concentric phase uses pin presses, dead-stop squats, eliminating stretch-shortening cycle
5. **Supramaximal eccentric loading** -- Using weight releasers or partner-assisted eccentric overload (100-120% 1RM eccentric, rack at bottom)
6. **Phase names: Eccentric / Isometric / Concentric** -- These specific phase names are unique identifiers
7. **2-3 week phases (very short)** -- Each contraction-type block is only 2-3 weeks, shorter than most block periodization
8. **Plyometric/reactive work paired with strength in each phase** -- Each phase includes reactive/plyometric counterparts to the strength work

### 10.8 Hard Exclusion Rules

- **DISQUALIFIED if:** No prescribed tempo (especially slow eccentrics) appears in the program
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Phase names/structure don't reference contraction types (eccentric, isometric, concentric)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** All sets use normal tempo (no slow eccentrics, no paused reps, no dead-stop work)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Rep ranges exceed 6 for main lifts (Triphasic uses 1-4 reps primarily)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Phases are longer than 4 weeks (Triphasic uses 2-3 week phases)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Hypertrophy (8-12 rep) work is a primary phase focus (Triphasic is about force production qualities, not hypertrophy)
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Program doesn't include plyometric/reactive training alongside strength work
- **DISQUALIFIED if:** Intensity is below 75% for main movements in any phase (Triphasic trains heavy throughout)

### 10.9 Typical Block/Phase Structure

| Phase | Duration | Contraction Focus | Tempo | Load | Key Method |
|-------|----------|-------------------|-------|------|------------|
| Eccentric | 2-3 weeks | Slow eccentric (5-6 sec lowering) | 6/0/1/0 | 80-90% (eccentric) or 100-120% (supramaximal eccentric with releasers) | Weight releasers, partner eccentric overload |
| Isometric | 2-3 weeks | Pause at sticking point (3-6 sec) | 3/6/1/0 (pause at bottom or mid-range) | 80-90% | Pin squats with pause, pause bench, pause deadlift |
| Concentric/Reactive | 2-3 weeks | Explosive concentric from dead stop | 1/0/X/0 (X = explosive) | 85-95% | Pin presses, dead-stop squats, block pulls, reactive jumps |
| Plyometric Integration | Concurrent | Reactive/elastic power | Reactive | Body weight to light load | Depth jumps, reactive bounds, medicine ball throws |
| Transition/Recovery | 3-7 days | General fitness, active recovery | Normal | 50-60% | Movement prep, GPP |

**Full triphasic cycle:** 6-9 weeks (Eccentric + Isometric + Concentric)
**Off-season for athletes:** 2-3 complete triphasic cycles (12-27 weeks)

### 10.10 Common Variations

- **Pure Triphasic (Dietz):** Strict Ecc -> Iso -> Con phases, 2-3 weeks each, with prescribed tempo and reactive counterparts. Uses stress charts for load management.
- **Simplified Triphasic:** Coaches remove supramaximal eccentric tools (no weight releasers) but maintain slow eccentric emphasis. Pauses may be shorter (2-3 sec vs 5-6 sec).
- **Triphasic for Team Sports:** Phases aligned with sport calendar. Eccentric and isometric in off-season, concentric/reactive in pre-season and in-season. Phases may be shortened to 2 weeks.
- **Triphasic + DUP hybrid:** Within each triphasic phase, daily rep ranges undulate (heavy/medium/light) while maintaining the contraction-type emphasis.
- **Triphasic Upper/Lower Split:** Separate triphasic progression for upper and lower body, potentially offset by 1-2 weeks.

## Detection Algorithm Notes

### Recommended Matching Approach

1. **First Pass: Keyword/Term Scanning**
   - Scan for methodology-specific terminology:
     - "tempo runs," "CNS," "high-low" -> Francis
     - "base building," "aerobic conditioning," "hill circuits" -> Lydiard
     - "Zone 1/2/3," "VT1/VT2," "80/20" -> Polarized
     - "threshold," "lactate," "mmol/L," "double threshold" -> Norwegian
     - "accumulation," "transmutation," "realization" -> Block
     - "hypertrophy phase," "strength phase," "peaking phase" (in sequence) -> Linear
     - "hypertrophy day," "strength day," "power day" (within same week) -> DUP
     - "max effort," "dynamic effort," "accommodating resistance," "box squat" -> Westside
     - "training max," "5/3/1," "AMRAP," "FSL," "BBB" -> 5/3/1
     - "eccentric phase," "isometric phase," "concentric phase," tempo prescriptions -> Triphasic

2. **Second Pass: Structural Pattern Matching**
   - Calculate intensity distribution across all sessions
   - Identify phase/block duration patterns
   - Detect rep range progression direction (decreasing = linear, constant = DUP, block-shifting = block)
   - Check for exercise variation (same exercises = 5/3/1/DUP, rotating = Westside)
   - Calculate sessions per week frequency

3. **Third Pass: Exclusion Filtering**
   - Apply hard exclusion rules to eliminate impossible matches
   - A program with medium-intensity sessions CANNOT be Francis
   - A program with distinct multi-week phases CANNOT be DUP
   - A program with >5 reps on main lifts regularly CANNOT be Westside

4. **Confidence Scoring**
   - Primary discriminator match: +30 points
   - Secondary discriminator match: +10 points
   - Exclusion rule violation: -50 points (hard disqualification)
   - Threshold for identification: 60+ points = "likely," 80+ points = "confident," 100+ points = "definitive"

### Key Domain Separators

Before running the full algorithm, classify the program DOMAIN first:

- **Endurance/Running programs:** Choose among Lydiard, Polarized, Norwegian
- **Sprint/Power programs:** Start with Francis
- **Strength/Powerlifting programs:** Choose among Block, Linear, DUP, Westside, 5/3/1, Triphasic
- **Mixed/Team Sport programs:** May combine elements; look for dominant methodology pattern

### Handling Hybrid Programs

Many real-world programs blend methodologies. When no single methodology scores above 60 points:

1. Report "Hybrid/Mixed methodology" with top 2-3 methodology influences
2. Example: "Primarily Block Periodization (55 points) with DUP elements within blocks (40 points)"
3. Common hybrids to recognize:
   - Linear + DUP = "Linear with daily undulation" (phases exist but weekly variation within)
   - Westside + 5/3/1 = "Conjugate accessories with 5/3/1 progression" (5/3/1 percentages but Westside exercise selection)
   - Polarized + Norwegian = "Modified Polarized with threshold emphasis" (mostly Zone 1 but Zone 2 > 10%)
   - Block + Triphasic = "Contraction-type block periodization" (A-T-R structure with eccentric/iso/con focus)
